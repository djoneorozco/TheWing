A calculator gathers the relevant inputs: for example rank, years of service, duty location, dependency status, income, home price, down payment, debt, and expenses. Normalization code converts these into the fields a module expects. The BAH tool requests the military calculation service; the mortgage page requests the mortgage service. 2. Military pay and official-source tables

BAH Location + pay grade + dependency

BAS Officer or enlisted category

Supported active-duty compensation is built from basic pay + BAH + BAS. Basic pay uses rank and creditable service; BAH uses supported location, pay grade, and dependency inputs; BAS uses the supported officer or enlisted category. The reviewed modules store 2026 tables. They are versioned datasets, not a guarantee of a fresh government lookup on every request. Additional income, retirement, or VA amounts depend on the selected scenario. Compare modeled gross income with actual take-home pay before budgeting.

3. Retirement and VA estimates
Monthly pension = high-36 average monthly basic pay × credited years × the applicable multiplier.
Multipliers used by the regular active-duty retirement module
System Multiplier
High-3 2.5%
BRS 2.0%

The regular active-duty retirement module supports High-3 and BRS. Monthly pension = high-36 average monthly basic pay × credited years × 2.5% for High-3, or × 2.0% for BRS. An entered 36-month basic-pay history can supply the average; without it, the current basic-pay proxy is an estimate. BAH and BAS are excluded from that pension base. This module does not cover every reserve, disability, or legacy retirement case. VA compensation uses rating and supported dependent categories; home-loan calculations use separate funding-fee and residual-income rules. These estimates do not determine an award, entitlement, or lender approval.

Original scoring explanation

The shared engine starts at 100. Missing/nonpositive income subtracts 45; missing/nonpositive housing subtracts 15. Housing ratio: over 40% subtract 28; otherwise over 35% subtract 18; otherwise over 30% subtract 10; above 0% through 28% add 3. Debt ratio: over 20% subtract 18; otherwise over 12% subtract 10; 8% or less adds 3. Total-load ratio: over 90% subtract 30; otherwise over 80% subtract 20; otherwise over 70% subtract 10; above 0% through 65% adds 4. Remaining-income ratio: negative subtract 35; otherwise below 8% subtract 15; 20% or more adds 5. Savings/home price: at least 10% adds 4; above zero but below 3% subtracts 6. A positive credit score below 620 subtracts 16; 700 or more adds 3. Unlisted ranges have no adjustment. The result is rounded and clamped to 0–100. These rules can produce counterintuitive results with incomplete inputs, so a grade must be read with its missing-input warnings.

7. What happens when something is missing
Incomplete inputs Warnings, missing-input lists, unavailable fields, or partial explanations can appear.
Module or AI failure Some modules can fail while others continue; other errors stop the request. A deterministic summary may replace an unavailable AI explanation.
Fallback results A returned result does not prove completeness. An absent cost is not a confirmed zero.

Missing or unsupported inputs can produce warnings, missing-input lists, unavailable fields, or a partial explanation. Some optional modules are allowed to fail while other results continue; other errors can stop the request. The published mortgage page includes a local calculation fallback with different rate assumptions. If the AI service is unavailable, the Amy gateway can return a deterministic summary. A fallback is not proof that every result is complete: check the displayed assumptions and inputs, and avoid treating an absent cost as a confirmed zero.

8. What a readiness result means

A planning indicator is not a probability of default, credit score, or lending decision.

A score or buy/wait label is a rule-based planning indicator. It is not a calibrated probability of default, a prediction of future behavior, an official credit score, or a lending decision. The monthly housing estimate excludes costs such as maintenance, utilities, moving costs, and closing cash unless a separate tool or entered expense includes them. Rates, insurance, taxes, and HOA costs need property-specific confirmation. A suggested buyer range on the published mortgage page is a heuristic around the selected price, not a fully solved maximum affordable purchase price.

The full explanation, one topic at a time

Open a topic for the formulas, assumptions, and limits behind the visuals.

1. Loan balance, down payment, and VA funding fees

Loan balance: when a percentage is supplied, down payment = home price × (down-payment percentage ÷ 100). A supplied dollar amount takes priority in the shared engine.

Base loan amount = home price − down payment.

For supported VA scenarios, the mortgage endpoint can add a financed VA funding fee to the loan balance before calculating the payment. The fee depends on loan type, prior use, down payment, and exemption inputs.

2. Interest rates: entered values and planning assumptions

Interest assumptions: the shared engine uses a positive entered annual rate when available. Otherwise its credit-score planning rates are 6.10% (780+), 6.25% (760–779), 6.45% (740–759), 6.65% (720–739), 6.95% (700–719), 7.25% (680–699), 7.55% (660–679), 7.85% (640–659), or 8.25% (below 640). These are fixed assumptions, not current lender quotes. The field called APR is used as a payment interest rate; it is not a calculation of a fee-inclusive disclosed APR.

3. How the principal-and-interest payment is calculated

Monthly principal and interest use standard fixed-rate amortization:

r = annual interest rate expressed as a percentage ÷ 100 ÷ 12.

n = loan term in years × 12. The shared engine defaults to 30 years.

Monthly principal & interest = L × [r × (1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1], where L is the loan balance.

The payment helper supports L ÷ n at zero interest. However, the current input handler treats a zero rate override as missing and selects a planning rate; a 0% field entry should not be read as a supported zero-interest quote.

4. Property taxes, insurance, HOA, and PMI

Taxes and insurance: the shared engine prefers usable positive user inputs, then supported city data. Its annual fallbacks are 1.2% of home price for property tax and 0.5% for homeowners insurance. These are planning defaults. HOA uses a supplied or local value where available, otherwise zero.

Monthly property tax = annual property tax ÷ 12.

Monthly homeowners insurance = annual insurance cost ÷ 12.

Monthly housing estimate = principal & interest + property tax + homeowners insurance + HOA + applicable mortgage insurance.

Conventional PMI is estimated when the down payment is below 20% unless a positive monthly PMI amount is supplied. Annual planning rates on the loan balance are 0.35% for 15–<20% down, 0.50% for 10–<15%, 0.70% for 5–<10%, and 0.90% below 5%. Credit below 680 adds 0.10 percentage points; below 640 adds another 0.15. Divide the annual amount by 12. The VA endpoint removes monthly PMI.

5. Monthly budget and the three ratios

Budget comparison: monthly non-housing load = entered living expenses + monthly debt obligations. Avoid counting the same payment in both fields.

Total monthly load = monthly housing estimate + living expenses + debt obligations.

Remaining monthly income = modeled monthly income − total monthly load. This is only as complete as the income and costs entered.

Housing ratio = monthly housing estimate ÷ modeled monthly income × 100.

Debt-only ratio = monthly debt obligations ÷ modeled monthly income × 100.

Total-load ratio = total monthly load ÷ modeled monthly income × 100. Because living expenses are included, this is not the same measure as a lender’s debt-to-income ratio. Gross-income estimates are not take-home pay.

6. Readiness scores and status bands

The shared affordability engine starts at 100 points and applies defined adjustments for housing load, debt, total expenses, remaining income, savings relative to home price, credit, and missing inputs. It rounds and limits the result to 0–100. It does not use the 26% / 28% / 22% / 12% / 12% weighting previously described on this page.

0–54 NO_GO
55–69 WATCH
70–84 CAUTION

Current shared-engine status bands are 85–100 Strong / GREEN, 70–84 CAUTION, 55–69 WATCH, and below 55 NO_GO. Letter grades run from A+ at 97 or above through F below 60. These are TheWing planning rules, not Department of the Air Force underwriting standards.

The interactive example above does not calculate a readiness score. These bands describe the shared engine’s output; they are not a prediction for the example.

TheWing’s mortgage estimate combines a repayment formula with entered costs, stored local data where available, and planning defaults. The current shared mortgage engine is version 1.1.0; the mortgage endpoint reports handler version 1.3.0. These calculations help compare scenarios; they do not establish loan eligibility.

The published mortgage page also has its own remaining-income labels: 35% or more Strong, 20–<35% Stable, 10–<20% Stressed, 0–<10% High Risk, and below zero Not Ready. It can use a browser fallback with different interest assumptions. Older dashboard grades use different formulas, so results across pages are not interchangeable. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Amy calculate my mortgage or military pay?
The calculator code produces those results from inputs, tables, and defined formulas. Amy is instructed to explain the available results. AI explanations can still contain errors, so check the calculator output and cited official sources.
Are the interest rates and readiness scores official?
No. The interest-rate bands, expense defaults, and readiness thresholds are TheWing planning assumptions. Military and VA tables have their own official sources, rate years, and eligibility rules.
Why do two tools show different grades?
Published pages currently use different calculation and scoring versions. Check the income basis, housing costs, missing inputs, and whether a browser fallback was used. Compare scenarios within the same tool.
What if information or a service is unavailable?
Some paths show missing inputs or warnings, reuse available results, or return a fallback summary. Other failures can stop the request. A partial result should not be read as a complete budget or a confirmed zero cost.
Does a strong score mean I should buy a home?
A strong score only means the entered scenario meets that model’s planning thresholds. It is not loan approval or a probability of repayment. Include your actual take-home pay, cash reserves, property costs, and personal plans before deciding.
